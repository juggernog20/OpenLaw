# Entity ownership registers, research notes

These notes collect what the primary sources say about the ownership register an entity-management product should keep for each kind of legal entity. They feed the design session that decides how the Ownership tab changes with the Entity type. Today the tab holds one register, the share register of ENT-011. The notes cover the common-law core: England and Wales, Delaware and the US uniform acts. They also cover UAE onshore law and the DIFC and ADGM free zones, with Jersey and Liechtenstein as reference points. Every claim links to the source it came from. All sources were read on 2026-09-30. Where a source did not answer a question, or a site blocked the fetch, the text says "unverified". Sections 2 to 10 are facts. The summary table in section 1 and all of section 11 are recommendations.

Seven findings update or contradict the brief.

1. UAE Cabinet Resolution No. 58 of 2020 is no longer in force. Cabinet Decision No. 109 of 2023, Art 22, abrogates it ([CD 109/2023, MoET English text](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Cabinet+Decision+109-2023+English+Version+06062024.pdf/f7138fc2-fe12-cef3-077b-b4c49c12eabd?t=1718181974877)).
2. UK companies and LLPs stopped keeping their own PSC register on 18 November 2025. UK LLPs also stopped keeping their own register of members on that date. UK companies still keep a register of members ([CA 2006 Part 21A Chapter 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/part/21A/chapter/3); [SI 2009/1804 reg 18](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/1804/regulation/18)).
3. The US Corporate Transparency Act no longer requires reports from companies formed in the US. A FinCEN final rule, 91 FR 52508, published and effective on 14 August 2026, made the March 2025 interim rule final ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16576/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision); [FinCEN BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi)).
4. The DIFC abolished its LLC form on 12 November 2018 and converted every DIFC LLC into a private company with shares. ADGM has no LLC form ([DIFC Companies Regulations 2018, regs 10.3 to 10.4](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/companies_regulations_2018.pdf); [ADGM Companies Regulations 2020, ss 2 to 3](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_20963_VER20241129.pdf)).
5. The DIFC Ultimate Beneficial Ownership Regulations do not cover trusts. A DIFC trustee keeps its records under Trust Law Art 60(6), for six years, not five ([DIFC Trust Law](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/trust_law_difc_law_no_4_of_2018_updated_2024_.pdf)).
6. None of the four foundation statutes researched requires the foundation to keep a register of beneficiaries. The DIFC Foundations Law does not use the word "beneficiary". It says "Qualified Recipient". The Jersey Trusts Law does not use the word "protector".
7. Some section numbers in the brief are wrong. The UK rule for companies without share capital is CA 2006 s 113(6), not s 113(3) to (5). The guarantee rule in the Companies (Registration) Regulations 2008 is reg 4, not reg 3. The ULPA "required information" section is s 108 in the 2013 text, not s 111. The UAE foreign-company articles are Arts 335 to 339, not 329 to 332.

## 2. Company limited by shares, the baseline

This section only anchors the comparison. OpenLaw already models it in ENT-011.

What the law requires. In the UK the register of members shows each member's name and address, the date the person became a member and the date the person ceased ([CA 2006 s 113(2), as enacted and in force](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/113/enacted)). For a company with share capital it also shows the shares held, identified by number and by class, and the amount paid on them ([s 113(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/113)). Joint holders count as one member ([s 113(5)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/113)). A single-member company records that fact and the date it happened ([s 123](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/123)). An entry for a former member may be removed after ten years ([s 121](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/121)). No notice of a trust may go on the register of a company in England and Wales or Northern Ireland ([s 126](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/126)). The Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 rewrites s 113 to use a service address and new ss 113A and 113B. legislation.gov.uk marks those changes as prospective ([s 113A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/113A)). The option to keep the register at Companies House ended in full on 26 January 2026 ([Part 8 Chapter 2A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/part/8/chapter/2A)).

In Delaware the stock ledger records every stockholder of record, the address and number of shares of each, and all issuances and transfers ([DGCL § 219(c)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc07/index.html)). The ledger may be electronic or on a distributed ledger if it converts to paper ([§ 224](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc07/index.html)). Shares are certificated unless the board resolves otherwise ([§ 158](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc05/index.html)).

The free zones follow the UK model. The ADGM register of members is the UK s 113 text ([ADGM Companies Regulations 2020 s 118](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/rulebook/118-register-members-0)). The DIFC "Register of Shareholders" adds three items. It records each date a shareholder's number of shares went up or down, the amount unpaid on partly paid shares, and a nominee shareholder who votes for joint holders ([DIFC Companies Law Art 44(1)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/companies_law.pdf)).

A UAE onshore LLC is a share company in all but name. Its capital "shall consist of shares equal in value", which the official consolidated English text calls stakes of equal value ([Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2021 Art 76(1)](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1542)). A 2025 amendment lets stakes form classes with different value, votes, redemption and priority rights, recorded in the Trade Register ([Art 76(4)](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1542)). The LLC keeps a register of partners at its head office. It records each partner's full name, nationality, date of birth and residence, and every transaction on the stakes with its date ([Art 74](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/376326/Commercial+Companies.pdf/12d14f53-1a3e-47b4-8e70-fac3f672c403?t=1645596097819)). An assignment binds the company and third parties only from its entry in the commercial register ([Art 79](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/376326/Commercial+Companies.pdf/12d14f53-1a3e-47b4-8e70-fac3f672c403?t=1645596097819)). The other partners have 30 days to buy a stake offered to a non-partner ([Art 80](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1542)).

As of a date. The register answers which Holder held how many shares of each class on that date. ENT-011 already does this by replay.

Columns the law names that ENT-011 does not hold today: the amount paid or unpaid per share, the UAE instrument date as distinct from the register date of a transfer, and the DIFC nominee who votes for joint holders.

## 3. Limited liability company

### What the law requires the LLC to keep

Delaware has one mandatory record. "A limited liability company shall maintain a current record that identifies the name and last known business, residence or mailing address of each member and manager" ([6 Del. C. § 18-305(h)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc03/index.html)). A member may demand more on a written demand with a proper purpose. The list includes the amount of cash and the agreed value of other property contributed by each member, the amount each member has agreed to contribute in the future, and "the date on which each became a member" ([§ 18-305(a)(3) and (a)(5)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc03/index.html)). In practice the LLC must be able to produce those facts. The records may sit on "distributed electronic networks or databases" ([§ 18-305(d)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc03/index.html)).

RULLCA gives members rights to information but does not list records the LLC must keep ([RULLCA § 410](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=0a06fb33-7fa0-0e41-f494-ba174e5dc9c7&forceDialog=0)).

In the UAE free zones the question does not arise. A DIFC LLC is now a private company with shares, and its membership interests became shares ([DIFC Companies Regulations 2018, regs 10.3 to 10.4](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/companies_regulations_2018.pdf)). ADGM offers companies limited by shares, limited by guarantee, or unlimited, and no LLC ([ADGM Companies Regulations 2020 s 2](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_20963_VER20241129.pdf)).

### Parties and roles

- Member. A person admitted under § 18-301 ([§ 18-101(13)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc01/index.html)). After formation a person is admitted as the LLC agreement provides, or by default "upon the consent of all members" ([§ 18-301(b)(1)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc03/index.html)). A person may be a member with no contribution and no LLC interest ([§ 18-301(d)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc03/index.html); [RULLCA § 401(d)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=0a06fb33-7fa0-0e41-f494-ba174e5dc9c7&forceDialog=0)).
- Assignee. An assignment does not make the assignee a member. The assignee takes the profits, losses and distributions assigned. The assignor stops being a member only when it assigns all of its interest ([§ 18-702(b)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc07/index.html)). An assignee becomes a member as the agreement provides, or by default with the consent of all members ([§ 18-704(a)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc07/index.html)). RULLCA calls this person a transferee, and a dissociated member keeps its interest "solely as a transferee" ([RULLCA §§ 102(25), 502, 603(a)(3)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=0a06fb33-7fa0-0e41-f494-ba174e5dc9c7&forceDialog=0)).
- Manager. A person named or designated as manager in the LLC agreement ([§ 18-101(12)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc01/index.html)). By default members manage in proportion to their interest in profits ([§ 18-402](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc04/index.html)). Under RULLCA an LLC is member-managed unless the agreement says otherwise, and a manager need not be a member ([RULLCA § 407](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=0a06fb33-7fa0-0e41-f494-ba174e5dc9c7&forceDialog=0)).
- Class or group of members. The agreement may create classes, including senior classes later, and voting "may be on a per capita, number, financial interest, class, group or any other basis" ([§ 18-302](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc03/index.html)).
- Series. The agreement may establish series of members, managers, interests or assets. A member stops being associated with a series when it assigns all of its interest in that series ([§ 18-215](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc02/index.html)).

### What measures the interest

The statute defines the LLC interest as economics only: "a member's share of the profits and losses ... and a member's right to receive distributions" ([§ 18-101(10)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc01/index.html)). It does not mention units or percentages. Units are market practice.

A contribution is cash, property, services, or a promissory note or other obligation to contribute ([§ 18-101(3)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc01/index.html)). A promise to contribute binds the member. An obligation "payable upon a discretionary call" is not enforceable before the call ([§ 18-502(a) and (b)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc05/index.html)).

The default shares differ by statute.

- Delaware. If the agreement is silent, profits, losses and distributions follow "the agreed value (as stated in the records of the limited liability company) of the contributions made by each member to the extent they have been received ... and have not been returned" ([§§ 18-503, 18-504](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc05/index.html)).
- RULLCA. Distributions before dissolution are "in equal shares among members and persons dissociated as members" ([RULLCA § 404(a)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=0a06fb33-7fa0-0e41-f494-ba174e5dc9c7&forceDialog=0)). On winding up, unreturned contributions come back first ([§ 707(b)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=0a06fb33-7fa0-0e41-f494-ba174e5dc9c7&forceDialog=0)).

Capital accounts come from US tax law, not LLC law. A capital account goes up by money and property contributed and income allocated, and down by distributions and losses allocated. A partner with several classes has "a single capital account" ([Treas. Reg. § 1.704-1(b)(2)(iv)(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/section-1.704-1)). A member's own note raises the capital account only when the member pays it ([§ 1.704-1(b)(2)(iv)(d)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/section-1.704-1)). The register cannot compute a capital account, because it does not hold profit and loss allocations.

Profits interests also come from tax law. A capital interest would give its holder a share of the proceeds if the partnership sold its assets at fair market value and liquidated at the time of receipt. A profits interest is any other partnership interest ([Rev. Proc. 2001-43 § 2, quoting Rev. Proc. 93-27](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-01-43.pdf)). For an unvested profits interest the test runs at grant ([Rev. Proc. 2001-43 § 4](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-01-43.pdf)). The safe harbor does not apply to a disposal within two years ([Notice 2005-43](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-05-43.pdf)). The "threshold" or "hurdle" value per unit is market practice. No IRS source read uses those words.

### Columns

Source tags: DE is the Delaware LLC Act, RU is RULLCA, TAX is IRS or Treasury, MKT is market practice.

- Holder name and last known address. DE § 18-305(h).
- Status: admitted member, or assignee with economic rights only. DE §§ 18-702, 18-704. RU § 603.
- Date admitted. DE § 18-305(a)(5).
- Date ceased, with the cause: resignation, assignment of the whole interest, bankruptcy, death, expulsion. DE §§ 18-304, 18-603, 18-702(b). RU § 602.
- Class or group, and series. DE §§ 18-302, 18-215.
- Units. MKT.
- Percentage, if the agreement states one. MKT. The default is derived as described above.
- Contribution committed: amount, form, agreed value, call terms. DE §§ 18-305(a)(5), 18-502.
- Contribution received and returned, with dates. DE §§ 18-503, 18-504. RU § 707(b).
- Profits interest: grant date, threshold at grant, vesting terms. TAX for the test, MKT for the threshold field.
- Certificate number, optional. DE § 18-702(c) allows certificates and bans bearer form. RU § 502(d).
- Pledge or charging order. DE §§ 18-702(b)(3), 18-703.

Managers are a separate list with appointment and cessation dates. They are officers, not holders. OpenLaw already keeps officers in `entity_officers` under ENT-001.

### Events

- Admission of a member, with or without an interest. DE § 18-301.
- Issue of an interest or units. DE § 18-301(b)(1). No pre-emptive rights by default under § 18-301(e).
- Commitment or subscription. DE §§ 18-502, 18-506.
- Capital call. DE § 18-502(b) makes the call its own event.
- Contribution received.
- Return of contribution, or distribution. DE §§ 18-503, 18-504. RU § 404.
- Default remedy: dilution, forfeiture, forced sale, redemption. DE § 18-502(c).
- Assignment, whole or part. DE § 18-702. RU § 502.
- Admission of an assignee. DE § 18-704.
- Redemption. By default redeemed interests are "deemed canceled". DE § 18-702(e).
- Resignation. A Delaware member may not resign before dissolution unless the agreement allows it. DE § 18-603. A RULLCA member may withdraw at any time. RU § 601.
- Dissociation by operation of law: death, bankruptcy, termination of an entity member. DE § 18-304. RU § 602.
- Profits-interest grant, vesting, forfeiture. TAX and MKT.
- Series established, member associated, series terminated. DE § 18-215.

### As of a date

On a date the register must say who was an admitted member, who was only an assignee, which class and series each held, how many units if any, and how much capital each had committed, contributed and got back. Under the Delaware default the percentages move with every contribution and every return. So a percentage on a date must come from replaying the money events, not from a stored figure.

### How it differs from the share register

- A holder may hold nothing, as a member without an interest, or hold economics without being a member, as an assignee.
- The unit may be money, not a count.
- Profit share, loss share, distribution share and votes can each follow a different basis.
- A commitment exists before the money arrives.
- Certificates are optional.
- The statute sets no authorized count.

Sources for each row follow.

UK general partnership. Unless the partners agree otherwise, partners share capital and profits equally ([Partnership Act 1890 s 24(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/53-54/39/section/24)). A new partner needs every partner's consent ([s 24(7)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/53-54/39/section/24)). The books stay at the place of business ([s 24(9)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/53-54/39/section/24)). An assignee takes only the share of profits and has no say in management ([s 31](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/53-54/39/section/31)). The US Uniform Partnership Act has the same equal-share default ([UPA §§ 401(a), 405(a)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=9e30c4d7-dbed-c173-dfc1-26394e60152d&forceDialog=0)). It requires books and records only "if any" ([UPA § 408(a)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=9e30c4d7-dbed-c173-dfc1-26394e60152d&forceDialog=0)).

UK limited partnership. A limited partnership has one or more general partners and one or more limited partners ([Limited Partnerships Act 1907 s 4(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/4)). A limited partner outside a private fund limited partnership contributes a stated sum on entry and is liable up to it ([s 4(2A)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/4)). A limited partner in a private fund limited partnership has no duty to contribute unless agreed ([s 4(2B)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/4)). A limited partner who manages becomes liable as a general partner ([s 6(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/6)). With the general partners' consent a limited partner may assign its share, and the assignee "shall become a limited partner" ([s 6(5)(b)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/6)). The registrar receives the partners' names and, outside a private fund, each limited partner's contribution ([s 8A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/8A)). Changes go to the registrar within 7 days, including a change between general and limited partner ([s 9(1A)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/9)). Outside a private fund, an assignment of a limited partner's share has no effect until it appears in the Gazette ([s 10(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/10)). The 2023 Act reforms add 14-day notices of new and departing partners and of changes in contributions ([ss 8R, 8S](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/8R)). The government's June 2026 report says those reforms still need secondary legislation and Companies House systems ([third ECCTA implementation report, s 4](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a28300ee371d9d2c0052b3e/third-report-on-the-implementation-and-operation-of-parts-1-to-3-of-the-economic-crim-and-corporate-transparency-act-2023.pdf)). Whether any commencement order came after June 2026 is unverified.

UK LLP. The first members are the subscribers. Later members join by agreement with the existing members ([Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2000 s 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/12/section/4)). Designated members are named in the incorporation document or designated later. If there would be fewer than two, every member is a designated member ([s 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/12/section/8)). The LLP notifies Companies House within 14 days when a person becomes or ceases to be a member ([SI 2009/1804 reg 17A, applying CA 2006 ss 167G to 167L](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/1804/regulation/17A)). The duty to keep a register of members ended on 18 November 2025 ([reg 18, omitted by SI 2025/1033](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/1804/regulation/18); [SI 2025/1033](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/1033/made)). By default all members share capital and profits equally, and no one joins or assigns an interest without every member's consent ([LLP Regulations 2001 reg 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/1090/regulation/7)).

Delaware limited partnership. The certificate names only the general partners ([6 Del. C. § 17-201(a)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c017/sc02/index.html)). "A limited partnership shall maintain a current record that identifies the name and last known business, residence, or mailing address of each partner" ([§ 17-305(g)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c017/sc03/index.html)). A limited partner may demand the contributions made and promised by each partner and the date each became a partner ([§ 17-305(a)(5)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c017/sc03/index.html)). A limited partner or a general partner may be admitted with no contribution and no interest ([§§ 17-301(d), 17-401](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c017/sc03/index.html)). The commitment and call rules match the LLC Act ([§ 17-502](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c017/sc05/index.html)). If the agreement is silent, profits and distributions follow the agreed value of contributions received and not returned, "as stated in the records of the limited partnership" ([§§ 17-503, 17-504](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c017/sc05/index.html)). A limited partner cannot withdraw before dissolution unless the agreement allows it ([§ 17-603](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c017/sc06/index.html)). The assignment rules match the LLC Act, and an assignee of a general partner becomes a limited partner when admitted ([§§ 17-702, 17-704](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c017/sc07/index.html)).

ULPA. The partnership keeps a current list of partners, with general and limited partners listed apart. Unless the agreement already states them, it also keeps the agreed value of contributions, the times or events for further contributions, and which interest a person holds as general partner and which as limited partner ([ULPA § 108](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=0c58b3b3-49fd-18b0-29a0-d10f070297f7&forceDialog=0)). Distributions follow the value of contributions "as stated in the required information" ([§ 503(a)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=0c58b3b3-49fd-18b0-29a0-d10f070297f7&forceDialog=0)).

DIFC. The general partners of a limited partnership keep an alphabetical register of partners with each partner's name, address, date registered, date ceased, and capacity as general or limited partner. They also keep "the amount of any contributions agreed to be made by partners and the time at which, or events on the happening of which, the contributions are to be made", and "the amount of money and nature and value of any other property contributed by each partner and the dates thereof" ([DIFC Limited Partnership Law Art 15(1)](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/limited_partnership_law_no_4_of_2006_consolidated.pdf)). An assignee does not become a limited partner "until the assignee's ownership of the assigned interest is entered into the register" ([Art 26(2)](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/limited_partnership_law_no_4_of_2006_consolidated.pdf)). The DIFC register of partners must hold the "amount and type of contribution including, if relevant, the percentage interests or the number and class of units or other rights held" ([DIFC Limited Partnership Regulations reg 3.1(g)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/limited_partnership_regulations_consolidated_2018-_done.pdf)). This is the only statute found that names a units or percentage column for a partnership. The DIFC general partnership and LLP registers hold names, addresses and dates, and no economics ([GP Regulations reg 3.1](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/general_partnership_regulations_consollidated_2018-_done.pdf); [LLP Regulations reg 3.1](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/limited_liability_partnership_regulations_consolidated_2018-_done.pdf)). A DIFC LLP must have at least one designated member ([DIFC LLP Law Art 23(2)](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/limited_liability_partnership_law_no_5_of_2004_consolidated.pdf)). The difc.com pages label these three consolidated laws "Inactive". The reason is unverified.

ADGM. The UK Limited Partnerships Act 1907 and Partnership Act 1890 apply in ADGM with modifications, and without the Gazette rule in s 10 ([Application of English Law Regulations 2015, Schedule](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_14585_VER121020.Application_of_English_Law_Regulations_2015.pdf.pdf)). In the ADGM text a limited partner may contribute at any time, and registration gives only the general partners' names ([ADGM Limited Partnerships Act 1907, ss 4 and 8A](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/13_Limited_Partnerships_Act_1907_updated_Dec2020.pdf)). An ADGM LLP must keep a register of its members and a register of members' residential addresses, and members share equally by default ([ADGM LLP Rules 2026, rule 12 and Part 3](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_30196_VER26062026.pdf)).

### Parties and roles

- General partner. Unlimited liability in a UK limited partnership. A Delaware general partner may hold no interest and contribute nothing. A DIFC general partner cannot also be a limited partner ([DIFC LP Law Art 11(1)](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/limited_partnership_law_no_4_of_2006_consolidated.pdf)). Under ULPA one person may be both, and the records must show which interest is held in which capacity.
- Limited partner.
- Partner in a general partnership.
- LLP member, with a designated member flag.
- Assignee or transferee, with economic rights only until admitted.

### What measures the interest

The statutes recognise contributions committed, contributions made with dates, contributions returned, and classes. A percentage or unit count is statutory only in the DIFC limited partnership register, and only "if relevant". Every other percentage comes from the partnership agreement. No statute requires stored percentages that sum to 100. The defaults are equal shares in the UK general partnership, the UK LLP, the UPA, the DIFC general partnership, the DIFC LLP and the ADGM LLP. The defaults are pro rata to contributed and unreturned capital in Delaware, under ULPA and in the DIFC limited partnership ([DIFC LP Law Art 31](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/limited_partnership_law_no_4_of_2006_consolidated.pdf)).

Fund-style limited partnerships show which money columns matter. The commitment is an obligation to contribute. It is enforceable only after a call. Contributed capital at agreed value drives the default profit share. Distributions and returns of capital reduce it. Remedies for a defaulting partner include dilution and forfeiture ([6 Del. C. §§ 17-101, 17-502 to 17-504](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c017/sc05/index.html)).

### Columns

- Partner or member name and address. Delaware § 17-305(g), DIFC Art 15(1).
- Capacity: general partner, limited partner, member, designated member. UK LPA s 9(1A), DIFC Art 15(1), LLPA 2000 s 8.
- Status: admitted, or assignee only. DIFC Art 26(2), Delaware § 17-704.
- Date admitted, date ceased. DIFC Art 15(1), Delaware § 17-305(a)(5).
- Class. Delaware § 17-302.
- Commitment, with call timing or events. Delaware § 17-305(a)(5), ULPA § 108, DIFC Art 15(1)(d).
- Contributions made, with dates and form. DIFC Art 15(1)(e), UK LPA s 8A(2).
- Contributions returned. UK LPA s 4(3), DIFC Art 32.
- Units or percentage, if the agreement uses them. DIFC LP Regulations reg 3.1(g).
- Certificate number, optional. Delaware § 17-702(b).

### Events

Admission, cessation, commitment, capital call, contribution, return of contribution, distribution, assignment in whole or part, pledge, admission of an assignee, change between general and limited partner, redemption, default and forfeiture, designation or de-designation of an LLP member. For a UK private fund limited partnership, the designation date also counts ([LPA 1907 s 8D](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Edw7/7/24/section/8D)).

### As of a date

As for an LLC: who was a partner in which capacity, who was only an assignee, and how much capital each had committed, contributed and got back on the date. Two jurisdictions tie legal effect to a record date. A DIFC assignee becomes a limited partner on entry in the register. A UK assignment of a limited partner's share takes effect on the Gazette notice.

### How it differs from the share register

The differences are the same as for the LLC. Partnerships add one more: a partner's capacity, general or limited, is part of the interest and changes by a dated event.

## 5. Trusts

### What the law requires

FATF. Trustees must "obtain and hold adequate, accurate, and up-to-date beneficial ownership information" on five kinds of party: the settlors, the trustees, any protectors, each beneficiary or the class of beneficiaries and objects of a power, and any other natural person exercising ultimate effective control ([FATF Recommendations, updated October 2025, INR.25 para 1](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/recommendations/FATF%20Recommendations%202012.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)). They must also hold basic information on the trust's other regulated agents and service providers, such as investment managers, accountants and tax advisers ([INR.25 para 1](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/recommendations/FATF%20Recommendations%202012.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)). Basic information on the trust itself is its name and any tax identifier, the trust deed and its purposes, and the trustee's residence or place of administration ([INR.25 footnote 83](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/recommendations/FATF%20Recommendations%202012.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)). For a class, the trustee need not collect full details on a person until that person becomes entitled at a payout ([INR.25 footnote 88](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/recommendations/FATF%20Recommendations%202012.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)). Records stay for at least five years after the trustee's involvement ends ([INR.25 para 8](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/recommendations/FATF%20Recommendations%202012.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)). The FATF glossary gives the same five-part list as the beneficial owner of a legal arrangement ([FATF glossary](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/pages/fatf-glossary.html)). The 2024 guidance says each record "must make clear whether this beneficial owner is the settlor, trustee, protector, beneficiary, or a natural person exercising ultimate effective control" ([FATF guidance on legal arrangements, March 2024, para 87](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Guidance-Beneficial-Ownership-Transparency-Legal-Arrangements.html)). It says there is "no universal definition" of a protector ([para 36](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Guidance-Beneficial-Ownership-Transparency-Legal-Arrangements.html)). It says persons named in a letter of wishes should in principle be treated as objects of a power ([para 48](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Guidance-Beneficial-Ownership-Transparency-Legal-Arrangements.html)).

UK Money Laundering Regulations 2017. The beneficial owners of a trust are the settlor, the trustees, the beneficiaries, the class of persons in whose main interest the trust operates where the individuals are not yet determined, and any individual who has control ([reg 6(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/6)). Control means a power, alone or with others, to deal with trust property, to vary or end the trust, to add or remove beneficiaries, to appoint or remove trustees, or to veto any of these ([reg 6(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/6)). Trustees "must maintain accurate and up-to-date records in writing of all the beneficial owners of the trust, and of any potential beneficiaries" named in a letter of wishes ([reg 44(1) and (5)(b)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/44)). A trustee may describe a class instead of naming its members ([reg 44(2)(b)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/44)). Trustees report a change "and the date on which it occurred" within 14 days to the firm they deal with ([reg 44(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/44)). Paid trustees keep the records for five years after the final distribution ([reg 44(9)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/44)). The register content is in [reg 45](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/45). For each individual it includes "the nature of the individual's role in relation to the trust" ([reg 45(6)(f)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/45)) and, for non-taxable trusts, "the nature and extent of the individual's beneficial interest" ([reg 45(10E)(c)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/45)).

UK Trust Registration Service. HMRC collects data on four kinds of party: settlor, trustees, beneficiaries, and "any individual who has control over the trust, in some instances referred to as protectors" ([TRSM32010](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32010)). The mandatory fields per individual are name, country of residence, nationality and date of birth. For an entity they are name and address ([TRSM32010](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32010)). The trust's own fields include its name, the date created, meaning "the date that assets were first settled into trust", and where its trustees and settlors are based ([TRSM32020](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32020)). One trustee is the lead trustee and gives contact details and an identity number ([TRSM32030](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32030)). A class may be used only if its members "cannot all reasonably be identified individually" ([TRSM32050](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32050)). HMRC looks at "the nature of the control ... not the title", and names protectors, advisers, enforcers and nominators as examples ([TRSM32060](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32060)). Trustees register within 90 days and keep details updated within 90 days of learning of a change ([TRSM40010](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm40010)). The TRS should hold every beneficial owner since the trust became liable to register, including those who have gone ([TRSM32010](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32010)). A trust closes on "the date that the last asset in the trust was shared out" ([TRSM95010](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm95010)).

US Uniform Trust Code. A beneficiary has "a present or future beneficial interest in a trust, vested or contingent", or holds a power of appointment ([UTC § 103(3)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=881a3ad6-ca48-cda4-aafe-5cb205d348d1&forceDialog=0)). Qualified beneficiaries are fixed "on the date the beneficiary's qualification is determined" ([§ 103(13)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=881a3ad6-ca48-cda4-aafe-5cb205d348d1&forceDialog=0)). Where several people contribute, each is "a settlor of the portion of the trust property attributable to that person's contribution" ([§ 103(15)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=881a3ad6-ca48-cda4-aafe-5cb205d348d1&forceDialog=0)). "A trustee shall keep adequate records of the administration of the trust" ([§ 810(a)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=881a3ad6-ca48-cda4-aafe-5cb205d348d1&forceDialog=0)). The trustee reports at least once a year with a list of trust assets ([§ 813(c)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=881a3ad6-ca48-cda4-aafe-5cb205d348d1&forceDialog=0)). The Uniform Directed Trust Act calls a protector a "trust director" when it holds a power of direction and is not a trustee ([UDTA § 2(9)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=04e5b80e-3b2e-e30b-99fe-03ab5ed4a7fe)).

DIFC Trust Law, DIFC Law No. 4 of 2018. A settlor provides trust property. A beneficiary is "a person entitled to benefit under a trust or in whose favour a discretion to distribute property held on trust may be exercised". A protector is any person other than the settlor who holds a power under Art 84. An enforcer acts for a purpose trust ([Schedule 1](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/trust_law_difc_law_no_4_of_2018_updated_2024_.pdf)). "A trustee shall keep accurate accounts and records of his trusteeship" ([Art 60(4)](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/trust_law_difc_law_no_4_of_2018_updated_2024_.pdf)). A trustee of an express trust must identify the ultimate beneficial owners of the settlor, enforcer, protector, beneficiaries and any other controller, must keep the names and contact details of agents and service providers, and must keep both "for a period of six (6) years from the date on which it ceased to act" ([Art 60(6)](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/trust_law_difc_law_no_4_of_2018_updated_2024_.pdf)). A beneficiary is named or is ascertainable by class or relationship, and the terms may allow adding or excluding beneficiaries ([Art 45](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/trust_law_difc_law_no_4_of_2018_updated_2024_.pdf)). Powers that may be reserved or granted include the power to appoint or remove trustees, protectors and enforcers, to add or exclude beneficiaries, and to change the governing law ([Art 84(2)](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/trust_law_difc_law_no_4_of_2018_updated_2024_.pdf)). The PDF prints article headings without numbers. The article numbers here come from counting the headings. The law's own cross-references agree with the count.

ADGM. ADGM has no trust code of its own. English common law and equity apply, with the Trustee Acts 1925 and 2000 in part and the Variation of Trusts Act 1958 ([Application of English Law Regulations 2015, s 1 and Schedule](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/rulebook/application-english-law-regulations)). The perpetuity rule is abolished ([s 4](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/rulebook/application-english-law-regulations)). A trust instrument may make trustee powers subject to the consent of the settlor "or some other person as protector". The settlor or protector may hold powers to appoint and remove trustees, add and exclude beneficiaries, and change the governing law ([Trusts (Special Provisions) Regulations 2016 s 6](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/rulebook/trusts-special-provisions-regulations-2016-1)). The beneficial owners of a trust are the settlor, the trustees, each beneficiary, "any protector or enforcer", the class of beneficiaries, and any other person with control ([Beneficial Ownership and Control Regulations 2022, Schedule 1 para 2(1)](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/rulebook/beneficial-ownership-and-control-regulations-2022)). For each natural person the record holds full and former names, addresses, date and place of birth, nationality, occupation, passport details, "the date on which that person became a beneficial owner", the date the person ceased, and "the grounds on which that person is considered to be a beneficial owner" ([s 2(4)(a)](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/rulebook/beneficial-ownership-and-control-regulations-2022)).

Jersey. The Trusts (Jersey) Law 1984 defines settlor and beneficiary in the same words as the DIFC law ([Art 1](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_11_1984.pdf)). It never uses the word "protector". Art 9A covers the same ground: the settlor may reserve, or grant to another person, powers to appoint or remove trustees, enforcers and beneficiaries and to change the proper law ([Art 9A](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_11_1984.pdf)). "A trustee shall keep accurate accounts and records of the trustee's trusteeship" ([Art 21(5)](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_11_1984.pdf)). A beneficiary's interest is movable property ([Art 10(10)](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_11_1984.pdf)). A class closes "when it is no longer possible for any other person to become a member" ([Art 36](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_11_1984.pdf)). A trustee's resignation "takes effect on the delivery of notice" ([Art 19](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_11_1984.pdf)).

UAE onshore. The UAE has a federal trust law, Federal Decree-Law No. 19 of 2020. I did not read it. Its record rules are unverified. For onshore companies, Cabinet Decision No. 109 of 2023 requires the register of shareholders to record a shareholder who acts as trustee or nominee, and the persons it represents ([CD 109/2023 Art 10(2) and (3)](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Cabinet+Decision+109-2023+English+Version+06062024.pdf/f7138fc2-fe12-cef3-077b-b4c49c12eabd?t=1718181974877)).

### Parties and roles

The sources agree on these roles:

- Settlor. Includes a person who adds property later, and an economic settlor behind a nominee ([FATF 2024 guidance para 24](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Guidance-Beneficial-Ownership-Transparency-Legal-Arrangements.html)).
- Trustee. The UK TRS adds a lead trustee.
- Protector. FATF and ADGM name it. The UK files it under "individual with control". Jersey has no word for it.
- Enforcer, for a purpose trust. DIFC, Jersey and ADGM name it.
- Beneficiary, of several kinds: named with a fixed interest, discretionary, contingent, in remainder, a class, an object of a power, a potential beneficiary named in a letter of wishes, a default taker.
- Other person with control.
- Agents and service providers, such as investment managers and advisers. FATF INR.25 and DIFC Art 60(6)(b) require their names. They are not beneficial owners.

One person may hold several roles. A settlor or trustee may also be a beneficiary ([Jersey Art 10(12)](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_11_1984.pdf); [DIFC Art 45(3)](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/trust_law_difc_law_no_4_of_2018_updated_2024_.pdf)).

### Does a trust have ownership in the percentage sense

No, with one exception. A discretionary beneficiary has no share. FATF describes an object of a power as a class member "for whom there is yet no identifiable trust property/fixed interest" ([FATF 2024 guidance para 46(a)](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Guidance-Beneficial-Ownership-Transparency-Legal-Arrangements.html)). The trustee decides "how much each beneficiary will receive" ([para 40](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Guidance-Beneficial-Ownership-Transparency-Legal-Arrangements.html)). HMRC says of an accumulation trust that "No beneficiary is entitled to the income" ([TSEM1566](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem1566)). The law describes a beneficiary's interest by its nature and extent, as present or future, vested or contingent. It does not use a count. The exception is a fixed-interest trust. There a deed may fix a share, and Jersey speaks of a "presumptive, contingent or vested share" ([Jersey Art 38](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_11_1984.pdf)). A unit trust divides the beneficial interest into units ([Jersey Art 1](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_11_1984.pdf)).

The settlor gives the property away. The trustee holds legal title but not for its own benefit. So no party "owns" the trust as a shareholder owns a company.

### Columns for the trust

Trust name, tax identifier, date established, governing law, country of tax residence, place of administration, trustee contact address, lead trustee, trust deed and letters of wishes as documents, agents and service providers, revocable or not, trust period, status and date closed. Sources: [MLR reg 45(5)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/45), [TRSM32020](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32020), [INR.25 footnote 83](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/recommendations/FATF%20Recommendations%202012.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf), [TRSM95010](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm95010).

### Columns for each party row

- Role, from the list above.
- Party kind: individual, legal entity, or class.
- For an individual: full and former names, date of birth, nationality, country of residence, residential address, identity number. A settlor may also have a date of death.
- For a legal entity: name, registered office, legal form, governing law, register and number. Its own beneficial owners link through ([INR.25 para 1](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/recommendations/FATF%20Recommendations%202012.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf); [DIFC Art 60(6)(a)](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/trust_law_difc_law_no_4_of_2018_updated_2024_.pdf)).
- For a class: a description, and for an employee class the approximate size ([TRSM32050](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32050)).
- Nature of the role or powers held ([MLR reg 45(6)(f)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/45)).
- Nature and extent of the interest, as text, with an optional fraction where the deed fixes one ([MLR reg 45(10E)(c)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/45)).
- Date became, date ceased, and the grounds ([ADGM BOC Regulations s 2(4)(a)](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/rulebook/beneficial-ownership-and-control-regulations-2022)).
- Source document.

### As of a date

On a date the register answers who held each role, including classes and objects of a power. It does not answer who held how much. The law supports a dated replay of role events. The UK wants "the date on which it occurred" for every change ([MLR reg 44(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/regulation/44)). ADGM records the dates a person became and ceased to be a beneficial owner. HMRC keeps every beneficial owner since the trust became liable to register, and keeps history for 10 years ([TRSM32110](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trust-registration-service-manual/trsm32110)). A person who has left must stay in the data with an end date for five years under FATF and six years in the DIFC.

### How it differs from the share register

- Parties hold roles, not quantities.
- A party may be a class with no named members.
- One party may hold several roles at once.
- Property flows in by settlement and out by distribution. Neither changes who "owns" the trust.
- There is no certificate.
- A trust holds its own assets through its trustee. In England and Wales a company's register of members cannot show the trust at all ([CA 2006 s 126](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/126)). In the UAE onshore the register must show it ([CD 109/2023 Art 10(2)](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Cabinet+Decision+109-2023+English+Version+06062024.pdf/f7138fc2-fe12-cef3-077b-b4c49c12eabd?t=1718181974877)).

### What the law requires the foundation to keep

- DIFC. Accounting records under the Companies Law rules ([Foundations Law Art 33](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)), and a Beneficial Ownership Register ([UBO Regulations reg 4.1](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/ultimate_beneficial_owners_regulations_updated_2024.pdf)). The Guardian keeps records of its guardianship for its term plus six years ([Art 23(4)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)). The Registrar's public Register names the Founders and Council members ([Art 35](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)). Changes in Council membership and of Guardian go to the Registrar within 30 days ([Arts 22(11), 23(14)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)). I found no duty on the foundation to keep a register of Council members or of Qualified Recipients.
- ADGM. Accounting records, including "a record of the Assets and liabilities", kept for 10 years ([Foundations Regulations ss 20, 21](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_19329_VER01012025.pdf)). A record of beneficial owners ([BOC Regulations s 2](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_25975_VER20260626.pdf)). The names and addresses of Councillors, Beneficiaries and the Guardian go to the Registrar in a Confidential Disclosure, not into a register the foundation keeps ([s 47](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_19329_VER01012025.pdf)).
- Jersey. At its business address the foundation keeps the charter and regulations, a register of council members' names and addresses, a record of the guardian's appointment, a register of everyone who has endowed it, and financial records ([Jersey Foundations Law Art 36](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_23_2009.pdf)). There is no register of beneficiaries.
- Liechtenstein. The council keeps records and an asset schedule ([PGR Art 552 § 26](https://www.gesetze.li/konso/pdf/1926004000)). An unregistered private foundation deposits a formation notice with the Amt für Justiz and notifies changes within 30 days ([§ 20](https://www.gesetze.li/konso/pdf/1926004000)).

### Does a foundation have ownership in the percentage sense

No. Every regime read says so in its own words.

- DIFC. The foundation does not hold its property on trust for anyone ([Art 10(3)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)). A Founder has no interest just because it endowed the foundation ([Art 27(4)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)). A Qualified Recipient has no right to or interest in the property, only a right to payments due under the By-laws ([Art 29(3)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)).
- ADGM. The assets have full legal and beneficial title in the foundation and are not a Beneficiary's until distributed ([s 32(1)](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_19329_VER01012025.pdf)).
- Jersey. A beneficiary "has no interest in the foundation's assets" ([Art 25(1)](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_23_2009.pdf)).
- Liechtenstein. A discretionary beneficiary gets a claim only when the council validly resolves on a distribution ([§ 7](https://www.gesetze.li/konso/pdf/1926004000)).
- Netherlands. A stichting "geen leden kent", it has no members ([BW Book 2 Art 285(1)](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0003045)).
- FATF. "Foundations have no owners and are controlled by a board" ([FATF guidance on legal persons, March 2023, pp. 21 to 22](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/guidance/Guidance-Beneficial-Ownership-Legal-Persons.pdf.coredownload.pdf)).

A fixed-share entitlement exists in the DIFC ([Art 29(2)(a)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)) and in Liechtenstein ([§ 6(1)](https://www.gesetze.li/konso/pdf/1926004000)). It is a right to distributions, not ownership. The only percentage in any foundation rule is the ADGM control test: 25 percent or more of the voting rights, or 10 percent for a DLT foundation ([BOC Regulations Schedule 1 para 3(3)](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_25975_VER20260626.pdf)).

### Columns and events

A party row needs the role, the dates in and out, and any reserved powers with their expiry. In the DIFC a Founder's reserved powers last for the Founder's life, or 50 years for a legal person, and then lapse ([Art 26](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)). A beneficiary row needs its kind: fixed, prospective, discretionary, default, or a charity or purpose. An endowment row needs the giver, the asset, the value and the date.

The events are establishment, initial endowment, further endowment, an undertaking to endow ([ADGM s 14](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_19329_VER01012025.pdf)), appointment and removal of Council members and the Guardian, change of registered agent or service provider, addition and exclusion of beneficiaries, a Council distribution resolution and payment, amendment of the Charter or By-laws, assignment of founder rights ([DIFC Art 10(6) to (7)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf); [Jersey Art 18(2)](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_23_2009.pdf)), lapse of reserved powers, migration in or out, conversion to or from a company, and dissolution. Founder rights cannot be assigned under the PGR ([§ 30](https://www.gesetze.li/konso/pdf/1926004000)). In Jersey a founder's rights vest in the guardian when the founder dies, unless the charter says otherwise ([Art 18(3)](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/PDFs/L_23_2009.pdf)).

One DIFC rule touches the share register. When a DIFC foundation converts into a company, the Articles of Continuation say which Qualified Recipients become shareholders and how many shares each holds ([Art 66B(2)(d)](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/foundations-law-difc-law-no-3-of-2018_updated.pdf)).

### As of a date and differences from the share register

As for a trust: who held each role on the date, and which contributions and distributions had happened by then. A foundation differs from a trust in one way that matters to OpenLaw. It is a legal person that holds its own assets. When a foundation holds shares in a subsidiary, the foundation itself is the Holder.

## 7. Entities with members but no shares

Company limited by guarantee. Each member undertakes to contribute up to a specified amount if the company is wound up while a member or within a year after ([CA 2006 ss 3(3), 11(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/11)). The register of members holds the s 113(2) items and, if there is more than one class of members, each member's class ([s 113(6)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/113)). The guarantee amount is not a register column. It sits in the statement of guarantee and usually in the articles. The model articles say "The liability of each member is limited to £1" ([Model articles for private companies limited by guarantee, art 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/3229/schedule/2)). A person becomes a member on an approved application. A member may withdraw on 7 days' notice, and "Membership is not transferable" ([arts 21 to 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/3229/schedule/2/paragraph/22)). gov.uk says a guarantor with more than 25 percent of the voting rights is a person with significant control ([gov.uk, guarantors](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/guarantors)). ADGM also has companies limited by guarantee, and a guarantee company there cannot have share capital ([ADGM Companies Regulations 2020 s 4](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_20963_VER20241129.pdf)).

Delaware non-stock corporation. The certificate states that the corporation may not issue stock. The certificate or bylaws set the conditions of membership, and there may be classes of members with full, limited or no votes ([DGCL § 102(a)(4)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc01/index.html)). The DGCL applies with members in place of stockholders and memberships in place of stock ([§ 114(a)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc01/index.html)). By default each member has one vote ([§ 215](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc07/index.html)).

Co-operative. A UK registered society keeps a register of members with each member's name, address, shares held and amount paid, other property held in the society such as loans, and the dates of entry and cessation ([Co-operative and Community Benefit Societies Act 2014 s 30](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/14/section/30)). No member may hold more than £100,000 of withdrawable shares ([s 24](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/14/section/24)). The rules say whether shares are transferable, withdrawable, or both ([s 14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/14/section/14)). A society's shares fit the share register. The share register would need a withdrawal entry and a per-member cap check.

As of a date. For guarantee and non-stock entities, who was a member of which class on the date. There is no quantity.

## 8. Entities with no owners of their own

Branch. A branch is the foreign company itself, registered locally. None of the sources gives a branch members of its own.

- UK. An overseas company delivers a return within one month of opening a UK establishment. The return names the company's directors and gives its issued share capital, but no list of members ([Overseas Companies Regulations 2009 regs 4 to 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/1801/part/2)).
- DIFC. A foreign company must register as a Recognised Company to do business in the DIFC. It files details of the Recognised Company's shareholders or members and its directors, and a yearly confirmation statement from its home jurisdiction ([DIFC Companies Law Arts 133 to 136](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/companies_law.pdf)).
- ADGM. Branches register under the commercial licensing rules. The application gives the full names and addresses of the parent's shareholders, unless the parent is listed ([Conditions of Licence and Branch Registration Rules 2023, rule 3(1)](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_23206_VER202303.pdf)).
- UAE onshore. A foreign company's branch or office enters the Foreign Companies Register. The branch "shall be deemed as its domicile in respect of its activity in the State" ([Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2021 Art 337](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/376326/Commercial+Companies.pdf/12d14f53-1a3e-47b4-8e70-fac3f672c403?t=1645596097819)). A representative office may only study markets and may not trade ([Art 339(1)](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/376326/Commercial+Companies.pdf/12d14f53-1a3e-47b4-8e70-fac3f672c403?t=1645596097819)).

Sole proprietorship. A UK sole trader's owner is "personally responsible for all of the debts of the business". The business registers for Self Assessment with HMRC, not with Companies House ([gov.uk, set up as a sole trader](https://www.gov.uk/set-up-sole-trader)). The UAE "sole proprietorship" is different. It is an LLC with one owner, and its name ends "Limited Liability (Sole Proprietorship)" ([Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2021 Arts 71(2), 72(1)](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/376326/Commercial+Companies.pdf/12d14f53-1a3e-47b4-8e70-fac3f672c403?t=1645596097819)). It belongs on the share register.

## 9. Beneficial ownership registers

### UK persons with significant control

A person has significant control over a company if the person holds more than 25 percent of the shares or of the votes, has the right to appoint or remove a majority of the board, or has significant influence or control. The same tests apply through a trust or firm ([CA 2006 Schedule 1A Part 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/schedule/1A)). The nature-of-control statements use three bands: more than 25 percent but not more than 50, more than 50 but less than 75, and 75 or more ([SI 2016/339 Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/339/schedule/2)). For an individual the particulars are name, service address, country of residence, nationality, date of birth, usual residential address, the date the person became registrable, and the nature of control ([s 790K](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/790K)). A relevant legal entity is recorded with its name, address, legal form, governing law and registration number.

Since 18 November 2025 companies and LLPs no longer keep their own PSC register ([CA 2006 Part 21A Chapter 3, omitted](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/part/21A/chapter/3); [SI 2025/1118](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/1118/made)). They deliver the information to Companies House within 14 days of confirming it ([s 790LA](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/790LA)). The Companies House guidance says the local register requirement "will be abolished" from that date ([PSC guidance, version 5](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691c9c97e39a085bda43ef2b/register-of-people-with-significant-control-guidance-for-registered-unregistered-companies.pdf)). A company must always file something, including a statement when it has no PSC or cannot identify one. Identity verification of PSCs started on the same date.

### UAE onshore: Cabinet Decision No. 109 of 2023

CD 109/2023 replaces CR 58/2020 ([Art 22](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Cabinet+Decision+109-2023+English+Version+06062024.pdf/f7138fc2-fe12-cef3-077b-b4c49c12eabd?t=1718181974877)). It applies to legal persons licensed in the UAE, including the commercial free zones. It does not apply in the financial free zones, which are the DIFC and ADGM ([Art 3](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Cabinet+Decision+109-2023+English+Version+06062024.pdf/f7138fc2-fe12-cef3-077b-b4c49c12eabd?t=1718181974877)).

- The real beneficiary owns or controls 25 percent or more of the capital or votes, directly or through a chain, or controls by other means. Failing that, it is the natural person in the most senior management post ([Art 5](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Cabinet+Decision+109-2023+English+Version+06062024.pdf/f7138fc2-fe12-cef3-077b-b4c49c12eabd?t=1718181974877)).
- The Beneficial Owner's Register holds full name, nationality, date and place of birth, residence or notice address, identity document number with country and dates, "the basis and date on which the person became Beneficial Owner", and the date ceased. It is updated within 15 days ([Art 8](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Cabinet+Decision+109-2023+English+Version+06062024.pdf/f7138fc2-fe12-cef3-077b-b4c49c12eabd?t=1718181974877)).
- The Register of Partners or Shareholders holds the number of shares, their categories and voting rights, and the date each holder acquired them. For individuals it adds name, nationality, address, place of birth, employer and a copy of the identity document. It records a holder who acts as trustee or nominee, and the persons it represents. It is updated within 15 days ([Art 10](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Cabinet+Decision+109-2023+English+Version+06062024.pdf/f7138fc2-fe12-cef3-077b-b4c49c12eabd?t=1718181974877)).
- A nominee board member tells the company within 15 days of taking the role ([Art 9](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Cabinet+Decision+109-2023+English+Version+06062024.pdf/f7138fc2-fe12-cef3-077b-b4c49c12eabd?t=1718181974877)).

The Register of Partners or Shareholders is a share register with extra columns. It is not a separate register kind.

### DIFC and ADGM

The DIFC UBO Regulations are made under the Operating Law. They apply to companies, partnerships, non-profit incorporated organisations and foundations, but not to trusts ([UBO Regulations, consolidated 8 March 2024, reg 1.1.1](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/ultimate_beneficial_owners_regulations_updated_2024.pdf)). The threshold is 25 percent of shares, other ownership interest or votes, or the right to appoint or remove most of the directors. For a foundation or partnership the test is significant control or influence, with no percentage. The fallbacks are the person on whose instructions the governing body acts, then each governing-body member ([reg 3.1](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/ultimate_beneficial_owners_regulations_updated_2024.pdf)). The entity keeps a Beneficial Ownership Register with name, residential and service address, date and place of birth, nationality, passport or ID details, and the dates the person became and ceased to be a UBO. Changes go on within 30 days ([reg 4.1](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/ultimate_beneficial_owners_regulations_updated_2024.pdf)). It also keeps a Register of Nominee Directors ([reg 5](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/ultimate_beneficial_owners_regulations_updated_2024.pdf)).

ADGM companies, LLPs, limited partnerships with legal personality, and foundations keep a record of beneficial owners with the fields listed in section 5, including "the grounds". They also keep a record of nominee directors. Changes go to the Registrar within 15 days ([ADGM Beneficial Ownership and Control Regulations 2022, consolidated June 2026, ss 2, 2A, 5](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_25975_VER20260626.pdf)). ADGM lists the roles that count for a trust and for a foundation, and looks through any legal person holding a role. The holdings of a spouse, children under 18 and anyone living with the person are treated as held jointly ([Schedule 1 paras 2, 3, 6(4)](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/sites/default/files/net_file_store/ADGM1547_25975_VER20260626.pdf)). The June 2026 consolidation renumbered some sections. Section numbers other than s 2 and Schedule 1 may differ between the April and June 2026 texts.

### US Corporate Transparency Act

As of 30 September 2026 only a foreign company registered to do business in a US state must report. FinCEN's final rule of 14 August 2026 "continues to exempt reporting companies from having to report the BOI of U.S. person beneficial owners" and exempts US company applicants ([91 FR 52508](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16576/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision)). FinCEN's page says "U.S. companies are exempt" ([fincen.gov/boi](https://www.fincen.gov/boi)). For a foreign reporting company, a beneficial owner has substantial control or owns or controls 25 percent or more. The report gives name, date of birth, residential address, an identifying document number and an image of the document ([31 CFR 1010.380(b) and (d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.380)). The status of the repeal bills H.R. 425 and S. 100 is unverified.

### EU

The EU moved from directives to a regulation. The 2015 directive treats a holding of more than 25 percent as an indication of ownership ([Directive 2015/849 Art 3(6)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2015/849/oj)). The Court of Justice struck down general public access to the registers on 22 November 2022 ([Joined Cases C-37/20 and C-601/20, WM and Sovim](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/62020CJ0037)). From 10 July 2027 the AML Regulation applies. It sets the threshold at 25 percent or more, lists the beneficial owners of express trusts and of foundations by role, and requires the nature and extent of each interest and the date held from ([Regulation 2024/1624 Arts 52, 57, 58, 62](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1624/oj)). The sixth directive gives access to persons with a legitimate interest ([Directive 2024/1640 Art 12](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/1640/oj)).

How the beneficial owner record relates to each register kind:

- Share register and interest register. The 25 percent tests read the percentage from the register and multiply it down a chain. Control by other means cannot come from the register and must be entered by hand.
- Member register. The UK test is more than 25 percent of the votes. With one vote per member, that means a member of an entity with fewer than four members.
- Role register. Every trust role is a beneficial owner under FATF, the UK, ADGM and the EU. The ADGM foundation roles are also beneficial owners. The DIFC foundation test is control, with no role list.
- No register. A branch has no beneficial owners of its own. They are the head office's.

## 10. How other products and open standards model it

Vendor pages below are a source only for how each product models ownership.

Beneficial Ownership Data Standard, version 0.4. A statement carries a `recordId`, a `recordType` of `entity`, `person` or `relationship`, and a `recordStatus` of `new`, `updated` or `closed`. A new fact about a record is a new statement with the same `recordId` ([BODS schema](https://github.com/openownership/data-standard/tree/main/schema); [information updates](https://github.com/openownership/data-standard/blob/main/docs/standard/modelling/information-updates.rst)). An entity's `entityType.type` is one of `registeredEntity`, `legalEntity`, `arrangement`, `anonymousEntity`, `unknownEntity`, `state`, `stateBody`. Its `subtype` is one of `governmentDepartment`, `stateAgency`, `other`, `trust`, `nomination`. There is no subtype for a foundation, partnership or LLC. An open issue proposes a new classification ([issue #472](https://github.com/openownership/data-standard/issues/472)). A relationship links a `subject` entity to an `interestedParty` with a list of `interests`. Each interest has `type`, `directOrIndirect`, `beneficialOwnershipOrControl`, `details`, `share` with `exact`, `minimum`, `maximum`, `exclusiveMinimum`, `exclusiveMaximum`, and `startDate` and `endDate`. There is no count, capital amount or class. The `interestType` codes are `shareholding`, `votingRights`, `appointmentOfBoard`, `otherInfluenceOrControl`, `seniorManagingOfficial`, `settlor`, `trustee`, `protector`, `beneficiaryOfLegalArrangement`, `rightsToSurplusAssetsOnDissolution`, `rightsToProfitOrIncome`, `rightsGrantedByContract`, `conditionalRightsGrantedByContract`, `controlViaCompanyRulesOrArticles`, `controlByLegalFramework`, `boardMember`, `boardChair`, `unknownInterest`, `unpublishedInterest`, `enjoymentAndUseOfAssets`, `rightToProfitOrIncomeFromAssets`, `nominee`, `nominator` ([relationship-record.json and codelists](https://github.com/openownership/data-standard/tree/main/schema)). A trust is an entity of type `arrangement` or `legalEntity` with subtype `trust`. Its parties link to it through relationships with the role codes. Shares a trust holds are a `shareholding` relationship from the company to the trust entity ([representing trusts](https://github.com/openownership/data-standard/blob/main/docs/standard/modelling/repr-trusts.rst)). That guidance page uses the code `beneficiary`, but the schema code is `beneficiaryOfLegalArrangement`.

Open Cap Table Format. OCF stores an immutable list of dated transactions and replays them to get the state on any date. A partial transfer consumes the old security and issues new ones through `resulting_security_ids` and `balance_security_id` ([OCF architecture](https://github.com/Open-Cap-Table-Coalition/Open-Cap-Format-OCF/blob/main/docs/explainers/Architecture.md)). Stock transactions include issuance, transfer, cancellation, repurchase, conversion, reissuance, retraction, acceptance and consolidation. `StockClass.class_type` is only `COMMON` or `PREFERRED`, and the `Issuer` object has no entity-type field ([OCF schema](https://github.com/Open-Cap-Table-Coalition/Open-Cap-Format-OCF/tree/main/schema)). OCF does not support LLC units, profits interests or partnership interests. The coalition "decided to scope the initial release to DE C-Corporations" ([issue #272](https://github.com/Open-Cap-Table-Coalition/Open-Cap-Format-OCF/issues/272)). An LLC support request was closed with "If we do a project to add LLC support, we can revisit" ([issue #502](https://github.com/Open-Cap-Table-Coalition/Open-Cap-Format-OCF/issues/502)).

Carta. Carta LLC models interest types with a name, a prefix, authorized units and "interest behaviors" such as vesting, invested capital and appreciation rights for profits interests ([create interest types](https://support.carta.com/kb/guide/en/how-to-create-issue-and-share-interests-in-carta-llc-tI1VY15z8i/Steps/3724554)). An issuance carries holder, units, issue date, issue price, invested capital, liquidation terms, vesting, and a "Threshold Type and Value. Also known as Hurdle Rate" ([same guide](https://support.carta.com/kb/guide/en/how-to-create-issue-and-share-interests-in-carta-llc-tI1VY15z8i/Steps/3724554)). Invested capital is tracked per interest as initial, returned and unreturned ([invested capital](https://support.carta.com/kb/guide/en/how-to-track-invested-capital-in-carta-llc-MR0uXFWLkR/Steps/3776322)). In 2026 Carta folds Carta LLC into its core share-class model. Interest types become share classes, and Carta says "The three-field invested capital model doesn't have a direct equivalent" ([upgrade guide](https://support.carta.com/kb/guide/en/navigating-your-upgrade-from-carta-llc-to-carta-Ff1GSKXyou/Steps/5733620)). Carta's fund product records commitments per partner with an effective date, and capital calls with a notice date and a due date ([capital calls](https://support.carta.com/kb/guide/en/how-to-request-capital-calls-and-distributions-8PDUUZZO4k/Steps/3724463); [commitments](https://support.carta.com/kb/guide/en/increasing-an-lps-commitment-kPH3yiRuVd/Steps/3724586)).

Diligent Entities. Partnerships are limited or "corporate" partnerships, and a US LLC is a corporate partnership. ## 11. What this means for OpenLaw

Everything in this section is a recommendation, not a fact from the sources.

### 11.1 Recommended register kinds

Five kinds cover the entity kinds in sections 2 to 8. Each Entity type in Settings maps to exactly one kind.

1. Share register. Keep it as ENT-011 built it. Map companies with shares, UAE onshore LLCs, DIFC and ADGM private companies, and co-operative societies with shares to it. Add three columns the law names: amount paid or unpaid, a register date separate from the instrument date, and a flag that a Holder holds as trustee or nominee with a link to the persons it represents. The last one is what UAE CD 109/2023 Art 10 needs.
2. Interest register. One kind for Delaware and RULLCA LLCs, general partnerships, limited partnerships and LLPs. The law treats them alike. The Delaware LLC Act and the Delaware limited partnership act have matching sections on records, contributions, allocations and assignment. What differs is the role vocabulary, which the Entity type can supply. Carta's own retreat to share classes, and Athennian's single engine, suggest the shapes are close.
3. Member register. Guarantee companies and non-stock corporations. Members by class with dates in and out, no quantity, no transfer. It could be the interest register with every money column empty. I recommend a separate kind anyway, because the user sees a different screen with no money columns, and the rule that membership is not transferable needs a code branch.
4. Role register. Trusts and foundations. Parties hold dated roles. Contributions in and distributions out are a ledger beside the roles. Trusts and foundations share one kind. Their roles line up: settlor with founder, trustee with council member, protector with guardian, beneficiary with Qualified Recipient.
5. No register. Branches and representative offices show a link to the head-office Entity. A sole proprietorship shows its proprietor.

The interest register needs two things the share register does not have. It needs a holder status of admitted member or assignee only, because an assignee holds economics without membership. It also needs money events that change the default percentage: commitment, call, contribution and return.

### 11.3 Trusts and foundations on the ownership chart

A trust does not have ownership in the percentage sense. A foundation does not either. Nobody owns them. The chart should draw no percentage edges into a trust or a foundation.

What the chart should draw:

- The trust or foundation as a node, like any other Entity.
- Ownership edges out of it, to the companies it holds, with their percentages from those companies' share registers.
- Role edges into it from its settlor or founder, trustees or council, protector or guardian, and beneficiaries. They need a different line style and a role label, and no percentage. They should not count toward the majority-holder spine, the ownership-total warning, or the cycle check.

A trust raises one more question the chart cannot avoid. In law a trust's shares are held by its trustee. A UK register cannot even name the trust. A foundation holds its own shares. I recommend that an OpenLaw share register may name the trust Entity as Holder, so the chart edge runs from the trust. The statutory export would then print the trustee's name "as trustee of" the trust. This needs a decision, listed below.

A unit trust is the exception. Its units sum to a whole. It would need a share register for the units and a role register for the trustee and manager.

### 11.4 Beneficial ownership

Keep the beneficial owner record out of the Ownership-tab register kinds. It is a layer across all of them, and FUTURE-FEATURES already parks it as "Beneficial-ownership look-through and PSC flags". When it lands, it can derive candidates: holders at or above 25 percent from the share and interest registers, multiplied down the chain, and every role holder from the role register. Control by other means, nominee arrangements and "no beneficial owner" statements must be entered by hand. The UK no longer asks companies to keep a PSC register of their own, so for UK Entities the record is working papers plus a Companies House filing log. For UAE onshore, DIFC and ADGM Entities it is a register the Entity must keep.

### 11.5 Open questions for the design session

1. Is one interest register right for both LLCs and partnerships, with the role vocabulary taken from the Entity type? Or does the owner want two kinds?
2. Which basis projects an interest register into Holdings: units, contributed and unreturned capital, a percentage stated by the agreement, or per capita? Is that a setting per Entity? The Delaware and RULLCA defaults differ, so the product cannot assume one.
3. How much money does the interest register track? The choices are none, stated percentages only; commitments and contributions only; or commitments, calls, contributions, returns and distributions. Capital accounts need profit and loss allocations and belong to the accountant, not the register.
4. Does an assignee who is not admitted count in the projected Holdings? The assignee has the economics but no vote.
5. Do profits interests, with thresholds and vesting, land with the interest register, or join the parked "Option pools and grants" row in FUTURE-FEATURES?
6. Does the member register project Holdings per capita, or project nothing? A sole-member guarantee company owned by another Entity is common, and per capita would draw it at 100 percent.
7. Are the role categories fixed, with labels the admin can rename per Entity type? Beneficial ownership derivation and chart drawing branch on the category, which argues for fixed categories under the configurable-over-fixed rule.
8. When a trust holds shares, is the Holder on the company's share register the trust Entity or the trustee? This decides the chart edge and the statutory export.
9. Does the role register include the distributions ledger now, or only roles and settlements?
10. Can one Entity carry two register kinds, as a unit trust needs? Or is the unit trust a later case?
11. A branch links to its head office. Is that a new kind of relationship between Entities, like GLEIF's `IS_INTERNATIONAL_BRANCH_OF`, or a Holding at 100 percent? A branch is not owned by its head office. It is the head office.
12. Does a sole proprietorship draw its proprietor as a 100 percent owner, or show the proprietor as a field?
13. Can an Entity type change its register kind after entries exist? ENT-009's archive guard is the nearest precedent.
14. CONTEXT.md says to avoid "member" for a Holder. The law calls LLC holders members and LLP holders members. What are the OpenLaw terms for a holder on each new register, and for an interest-register entry, a role entry and a role?
15. Where do identity details live? The beneficial owner columns include date of birth, passport numbers and home addresses. ENT-004 covers access to the Entity, not to personal data inside it.
16. Should filing deadlines feed the compliance calendar of ENT-006? Examples are UK limited partnership changes in 7 days, UK LLP changes in 14, UAE register changes in 15, DIFC changes in 30, and UK TRS updates in 90.
